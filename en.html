WakaruYo Privacy Policy

Effective date: 2026-10-20 · Applies to: WakaruYo 1.0

This policy explains where WakaruYo 1.0 keeps what you put into it, and when and where it sends anything. WakaruYo is made and run by one person. In this policy, "the developer" means that person.

In short

Your diary entries and memos are stored on this device, and the app does not send them to the developer.

  • There's no account. No sign-up, no sign-in.
  • The only AI the app's features use is the one on this device (Apple Intelligence). Version 1.0 has no feature in which the app sends your writing to an external AI. You can, though, hand a memo's text to another AI app such as ChatGPT yourself, with "Continue in ChatGPT" in the Memos tab, after checking what goes (section 3). Writing Tools and the ChatGPT extension, which iOS offers in text fields, aren't part of the app, and the app doesn't turn them on or use them (section 5).
  • Apart from device backups you set up in iOS and iOS Writing Tools you use yourself (section 5), data leaves this device only when a feature you turned on yourself (iCloud sync, weather, Calendar), or an export or "Continue in ChatGPT" you chose, needs it, and only as much as that needs.
  • iCloud sync starts out off. If you turn it on, your content is encrypted on this device before it's uploaded to your iCloud. Some information, such as each item's type and name, isn't encrypted (section 3).
  • No ads, no analytics tools, no tracking, and no third-party SDKs.

The rest of this policy explains exactly how far each of these statements holds. Where something couldn't be confirmed, this policy tries not to claim it.

1. There's no account

WakaruYo has no sign-up and no sign-in. The app doesn't ask for or receive your name, email address, phone number, date of birth, or gender.

Version 1.0 doesn't use any server run by the developer. That means nothing on the developer's side stores your diary entries, memos, or to-dos, and the developer has no way to open them or delete them for you. Your data is on your device, and also in your iCloud if you turn sync on.

2. What's stored on this device

What you create in the app is stored on this device, in WakaruYo's own storage. It's kept so you can come back to it, edit it, and organize it.

Diary

  • Entries: title, body, when it was written and last changed, the language it's written in, how it was written (keyboard, handwriting, and so on), whether it's a draft, whether it's set to "Keep on Device" or "Let Go After 24 Hours", and when it expires
  • Handwriting pages: the handwriting drawing file, the language recognition was tried in and how it went, and the recognized text, but only if you confirmed it as text
  • Photos and videos: the photo and video files and their thumbnails, size, order, the captions you wrote, when they were taken, whether they came from the camera or your photo library, and video length
  • Photo and video metadata: Only the time a photo or video was taken is read from the information recorded in it (for the camera, the moment you took it), and it's kept separately in the app's database, not in the file. Photos are re-saved as pixels only and videos are rebuilt without their metadata, so all other metadata, such as location (GPS), device, and camera settings, is removed before anything is stored.
  • Decorations and page style: emoji, stickers, where you placed them, and the page style
  • Weather (only if you turned it on): condition, temperature and feels-like temperature, chance of precipitation, humidity, day or night, city name, time observed, and the data source. Coordinates are not stored.
  • Summaries: the summary text, when it was made, and a fingerprint (a SHA-256 value, not the text) used to tell whether the entry has changed since
  • AI Look Back results: the summary, possible feelings, the look-back text, what it noticed, tags, suggested to-dos, the feelings and answers you chose, whether it's hidden, a safety mark that shows whether the AI saw something that may be weighing on you, how sure the AI said it was of its reading, when it was made, how it was made (by the on-device AI, or chosen by you), the model's name, and the entry's language. So you can tell which writing a result came from, the exact text the AI read (the beginning, for a long entry) is stored with it. A fingerprint of the entry's words at that time (a SHA-256 value, not the text) and whether only the beginning was read are stored too. If you choose the feelings yourself without AI, only those feelings, when you chose them, and the entry's language are stored.
  • Diary cards: card number, a short line, a small suggested action, the emotion family and specific emotion, how that emotion was chosen, and the other feelings you had confirmed when the card was given. Cards contain none of your entry's words.
  • Collection: the numbers of the cards you've collected, when you first and last met each one, and how many times

Memos and the Memos tab

  • Memos: text, the handwriting drawing file, recognized text you confirmed and its language, tags, and whether the memo is pinned
  • Thought notes and sources: title, body, kind, status, which memo or entry it came from, and quotes with the web addresses they came from
  • Links and structure notes: the links you made between notes and the reason for each, and structure notes with the one-line descriptions inside them
  • AI tidy-ups and "Think Further": the tidy-ups ("Think It Through with AI") and suggestions made by the on-device AI
  • Short Talks: what you and the on-device AI said to each other, and any reply you started but didn't send
  • Mind maps: your mind maps and the text on each branch
  • Recent searches: what you searched for in the Memos tab

Schedule tab

  • To-dos: title, note, date and time, end time, priority, whether it's done, reminder time, where it was made (an entry or a memo), a routine's repeat rule, days, and time, and the mark that ties together a plan placed on several days
  • SEE look-backs ("SEE · Looking Back on Today"): the date, "What Went Well", and "Next Time"
  • This week's goals: title, the week, whether it's done, order, and how many times it's been carried over to the next week

Settings and small records the app keeps

  • My Stickers and My Presets: sticker images (pixels only) and their sizes, and preset names and styles
  • Settings: appearance, color and mood, language, the look for new entries, accessibility settings, whether the weather and calendar features are on, which calendars to show and which one to put to-dos in, recently used emoji, and whether "Show A Year Ago Today" is on for the Today tab
  • Which guides you've seen: whether you've seen the first-run guide, which one-time tips (Apple's TipKit) you've seen or closed, and the app version in which you were asked for a review. They contain no text.
  • Calendar links: which to-do goes with which calendar event (the event's identifier and a SHA-256 fingerprint, not text)
  • ID lists: the IDs of entries that have already received a card and of entries the app has already tried to add weather to automatically, plus entries moved over from memos with the ID of the memo each came from. They contain only random IDs, never text.
  • Page previews: the small pictures shown in lists. Text is drawn only as bars, so none of your writing is in them.
  • Sync information (once you've turned on iCloud sync): fingerprints and iCloud record details used to know what's already been uploaded, encrypted items that haven't been applied yet, an ID that tells iCloud accounts apart, and a sync log (only steps, counts, and error codes, with no content)
  • Sync key (once you've turned on iCloud sync): one randomly generated key. It's kept in this device's keychain, and if iCloud Keychain is on, iCloud Keychain carries it to your other devices (see section 3).
  • Temporary files: When you choose or record a video, the original, with its original metadata, sits briefly in a temporary folder while it's processed, and is deleted when processing finishes. The app also empties this folder each time it starts. Export files from the Memos tab are deleted when you close the share sheet.

How it's protected

  • The database holding your entries, memos, and the rest; handwriting, photo, video, and sticker files; page previews; sync information; recent searches; and temporary export files are all stored with iOS's strongest file protection class (Complete Protection). While your device is locked, even the app can't open these files.
  • This protection is part of iOS Data Protection, so it works as intended when your device has a passcode set.
  • Settings, ID lists, calendar links, and the sync log are stored with iOS's default protection. None of them contain the text of your entries or memos. Temporary files for video processing also use default protection and are deleted when processing finishes.
  • The sync key is a keychain item that can be read once the device has been unlocked for the first time after it starts up.
  • Device backups: WakaruYo doesn't exclude any files from backups. If you use device backups (iCloud Backup or a backup to a computer), this app's data goes into that backup like any other app's, and comes along when you restore to a new device. The sync encryption in section 3 isn't applied to backups; a backup is protected the way Apple protects it. According to Apple, iCloud Backup is end-to-end encrypted only if you turn on "Advanced Data Protection for iCloud"; otherwise the keys to your backups are kept in Apple's data centers. A backup to a computer is encrypted only if you turn on "Encrypt local backup". Backups live in your Apple Account or on your computer, and the developer can't access them.

3. What leaves this device

The 1.0 app doesn't communicate with the developer's servers or with any external AI server. The cases below are the only times the app exchanges anything with the outside world, and each one happens because of a feature you turned on or something you tapped. Anything that may be sent when you use iOS Writing Tools or the ChatGPT extension in a text field is sent by iOS, not by the app, so it's covered separately in section 5.

iCloud sync (off by default)

It works only if you turn on "Sync with iCloud" under Settings > Sync Across Devices yourself. Before it turns on, a confirmation screen shows what will be uploaded. While it's off, the app doesn't connect to iCloud.

  • Where: the private storage in iCloud that belongs to your Apple Account. Your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account see it once WakaruYo's sync is turned on there too. What's uploaded uses your iCloud storage. It doesn't go to the developer's servers, and the developer can't open your private iCloud storage.
  • When: The app syncs only while it's open on screen. When sync starts, the app registers with Apple's push notification service so iCloud can tell it about changes made on your other devices. Such a notification may briefly wake the app, but syncing happens only while the app is on screen.
  • What's uploaded: entries set to "Keep on Device" (including photos and videos, handwriting and confirmed recognized text, captions and when photos were taken, decorations, page style, weather and city name, and summaries), memos and their handwriting, to-dos, SEE look-backs, this week's goals, the Collection, My Stickers, My Presets, and some settings (appearance, color, accessibility, and the look for new entries)
  • What's not uploaded: entries set to "Let Go After 24 Hours"; AI Look Back results and the text the AI read; diary cards; the Memos tab's thought notes, sources, links, structure notes, AI tidy-ups, "Think Further", Short Talks, and mind maps; recent searches; calendar links; the ID lists; and other settings (the language, "Draw with finger", the weather and calendar settings, and so on)
  • How: The app encrypts each item on this device with AES-GCM before uploading it, and encrypts files chunk by chunk. The key that opens them reaches your other devices only through your iCloud Keychain. According to Apple, iCloud Keychain is end-to-end encrypted. The developer doesn't have this key.
  • What Apple's servers can see: Your content is encrypted, but the information used to find and organize items isn't: things like each item's type (entry, memo, to-do, and so on), size, when it was saved, its encryption format, and a short name for the key (which reveals nothing about the key), plus the item's name. Most names are random IDs, but a Collection item's name includes the card number (which reveals the card's emotion family), a SEE look-back item's name includes the date, and a file item's name includes the kind of file (handwriting, photo, or sticker) and its format (jpg, mp4, and so on).
  • Deletions follow: While sync is on, an item you delete on this device is deleted from iCloud, and from your other devices the next time they sync. If its words were changed on another device in the meantime, that device keeps it and uploads it again, so it comes back to this device too.
  • If the words of the same entry or memo were changed on two devices, one version is kept separately as "Version from another device", so neither is thrown away without telling you.
  • If you switch an entry that's already been uploaded to "Let Go After 24 Hours", its iCloud copy is deleted right away, as long as sync is on.
  • Turning sync off leaves your data on this device and in iCloud as it is. How to delete it is explained in section 7.
  • How iCloud handles your data is governed by Apple's Privacy Policy and the iCloud Terms and Conditions.

Weather and city name (off by default)

This works only if you turn on "Add the Weather to New Entries" in Settings yourself. The app asks for location permission only when you turn this on.

  • When: when you open an entry that has no weather yet and that this device hasn't tried before (usually when you start a new entry; an entry synced from another device counts too), and when you tap "Add the Weather Now". In both cases, the weather is fetched only if the entry's time is within an hour of now.
  • How precise the location is: The app asks iOS for a reduced-accuracy location, rounds its latitude and longitude again to one decimal place (about 11 km, or 7 miles), and uses only that.
  • Where it goes: The rounded location goes to Apple WeatherKit (for the weather) and to Apple's map service that finds the city name for a location. It doesn't go to the developer.
  • When an entry with weather is shown, the Apple Weather mark and logo image are loaded from Apple. This also applies to weather that came with an entry synced from another device, so it can happen even if weather is off on this device. None of your content is sent when this happens.
  • What's kept: the weather details and city name listed in section 2. No coordinates are stored, not even rounded ones. Weather is part of the entry, so if iCloud sync is on, it's encrypted and uploaded along with the entry.
  • If the weather can't be fetched (permission denied, offline, and so on), the entry is still saved, just without weather.
  • How Apple handles these requests is governed by Apple's Privacy Policy.

Calendar (off by default)

Only what you turn on under "Calendar" in Settings works. iOS asks for permission when you turn something on, and if you decline, the app works as usual. The app handles calendars only through this device's iOS calendar features and never connects directly to a calendar service (iCloud, Google, and so on).

  • Seeing events ("Show Calendar Events on Today and in the Diary", Full Access): Events are read only to show that day's events on screen and, as described below, to check for the same event before a to-do is added, then discarded. They're never stored, synced, or given to the AI. You can choose which calendars appear.
  • Adding to-dos to your calendar: This happens only when you tap "Add to Calendar" on a to-do (it appears once "Let To Dos Go into the Calendar" is on in Settings), or turn on "Also Add to Calendar" when copying a plan to other days. You can choose the calendar under "Calendar for To Dos".
  • Only the to-do's title, date, and time (start and end) go into the calendar. Its note doesn't. A to-do with only a note and no title takes the note's first line as its title when you close it, so that line goes in as the title.
  • With Full Access, the app first checks whether an event with the same title and time already exists, and if it does, doesn't create another. Editing or deleting the to-do updates or removes the event too, and you can take it out with "Remove from Calendar".
  • With "Add Events Only" access, events can only be added, to the default calendar, and later changes don't reach the calendar.
  • Diary entries, memos, and look-backs are never written to your calendar.
  • An event added to a calendar is stored by that calendar's account (iCloud, Google, and so on) without WakaruYo's encryption, under that account provider's policies. The event stays in the calendar even if you delete the app.

Notifications

  • iOS asks for notification permission the first time you turn on a reminder for a to-do.
  • To-do reminders are scheduled on this device. They aren't sent from a server.
  • A reminder shows the to-do's title. If there's no title, it shows the first line of the to-do's note. Whether the content appears on the Lock Screen depends on your iOS notification preview setting.

Dictate

  • The microphone and speech recognition are used when you tap "Dictate" in an entry or memo.
  • Turning speech into text happens on this device only. For languages or devices that can't do this on the device, Dictate can't be used. Rather than send your voice off the device, the app doesn't offer the feature.
  • No recording file is created. The transcribed text is treated exactly like text you type.
  • The first time you use a language, Apple's on-device speech model may be downloaded once. Your voice isn't sent when that happens.
  • The speech recognition permission prompt from iOS may include Apple's general notice that speech data may be sent to Apple. iOS adds that wording, and apps can't change it. WakaruYo asks only for on-device recognition.

Camera and photos

  • The camera is used only when you take a photo or video to add to an entry. When you record video, the microphone is used too, for sound.
  • When you choose from your photo library (adding photos or videos to an entry, or "Make from Photo" for a sticker), the app uses the iOS photo picker. Only the photos and videos you pick are passed to the app, so it never asks for access to your whole photo library.
  • The app doesn't save to your photo library or change the originals there. Inside the app, it stores only copies with the metadata removed, as described in section 2. Stickers are stored as pixels only too.

Export (Memos tab)

  • A file is created only when you tap "Export" in the Memos tab. "Connected Notes · Markdown" contains your thought notes. "Everything in Memos · JSON" contains memos, thought notes, links, structure notes, AI tidy-ups and suggestions, Short Talks, and mind maps. If a memo was moved into a diary entry, that entry's ID, title, and creation date are included too. You can also share a single note as Markdown.
  • The file is handed to the iOS share sheet, and you choose where it goes. Once it's sent, WakaruYo no longer protects it, and it's subject to the policies of wherever you sent it.
  • Version 1.0 has no way to export diary entries.

Continuing in ChatGPT or another app (Memos tab)

If you want to keep thinking about a memo's idea in another AI app such as ChatGPT, you can hand it to that app yourself. This isn't the app connecting to ChatGPT or OpenAI: it's a share you make, to an app you choose.

  • When: Tapping "Continue in ChatGPT" in "A Short Talk About This Memo" or in a mind-map branch's menu shows a confirmation screen, "Continue in ChatGPT?". Something is handed over only when you tap "Open Share Sheet" or "Copy Only" there; until then, nothing is handed over or copied. The confirmation screen appears every time you hand something over.
  • What: Only what you check on the confirmation screen, exactly as the preview shows it. You can choose the memo's text (including handwriting you confirmed as text), the Short Talk so far, and, if you started from a branch, the text of that branch and the branches above it (the branch path). A short request to develop the idea with you comes first. You can edit the preview's text before handing it over. Handwriting images, photos, other memos, and diary entries are never handed over.
  • Where: "Open Share Sheet" hands the text to the iOS share sheet, and you choose the app that receives it (for example, the ChatGPT app). It goes as text; no file is made. "Copy Only" puts the text on this device's clipboard, and doesn't send it to Universal Clipboard, which shares with your other devices. The app doesn't connect to ChatGPT or OpenAI itself, and never puts your text in a web address.
  • The developer receives nothing: What you hand over doesn't come to the developer.
  • After it's handed over: WakaruYo no longer protects it, and it's subject to the policies of the app that received it. If you hand it to ChatGPT, OpenAI handles it under your ChatGPT account settings and OpenAI's Privacy Policy. Copied text stays on this device's clipboard until you copy something else.
  • No record: The app doesn't record what you handed over, when, or to which app. The text on the confirmation screen is gone when you close it.
  • Bringing the answer back: You paste ChatGPT's answer yourself in "Bring Back ChatGPT's Answer". The app reads the clipboard only when you tap the iOS Paste button. Pasted text is saved on this device only when you keep it with "+ To a Branch" or "+ To a Thought Note", marked "From another app", and is then treated like the mind maps and thought notes in section 2.
  • Version 1.0 has no way to hand diary entries to another app.

Other

  • A web address saved in a thought note opens in your browser only when you tap it. So do the links on the policy screen (the web version of this policy, OpenAI's policy in section 3, and Apple's pages in section 5).
  • The crisis support information (section 10) shows a list built into the app and doesn't connect to the internet.
  • Downloading and managing the on-device AI models and speech models is handled by Apple, under Apple's terms.

What Apple shares with the developer

As with any app installed from the App Store, Apple shows the developer aggregate App Store statistics such as download counts, and may pass along usage data and crash reports from people who have chosen on their device to share them with app developers. If you use iCloud sync, Apple also shows the developer iCloud usage information for the app, without any of your content: overall figures such as the numbers of requests and errors, and a log of the past week's requests that shows, for each one, an anonymous iCloud user ID that's different for each app, the platform and OS version, and the kind of operation. Apple does this. The WakaruYo app contains no code for it. You can change whether you share usage data and crash reports with "Share With App Developers" in iOS Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements.

4. On-device AI

WakaruYo's AI features run on Apple Intelligence's on-device model.

  • Where it's used: looking back on an entry ("Look Back on This Entry"), summarizing an entry ("Summarize This Entry"), the memo features "Think It Through with AI", "Think Further", and "Short Talk", and "Find Corrections" in "Check What Was Heard". Each one runs only when you tap it.
  • What the AI is given: For looking back and summaries, the entry's title, body, and any handwriting you confirmed as text (for a long entry, about the first 4,000 characters of the body and of the handwriting text). For the memo AI, the memo's text (including handwriting you confirmed as text), what's been said in the Short Talk, and, when you ask from a mind-map branch, the text of that branch and the branches above it. For "Find Corrections", only the passage you just dictated. Photos, videos, location, and weather are never given to it.
  • Processed on the device: These features are handled by the on-device model, and this part of the app has no code that sends your writing over the network. Downloading and managing the model, and Apple Intelligence's own processing, are governed by Apple's terms and Privacy Policy.
  • When it's available: only on iOS 26 or later, with Apple Intelligence turned on, on a device that supports it, and in a supported language. Otherwise the AI features aren't available, and you can choose an entry's feeling yourself.
  • Results: stored on this device as described in section 2. Summaries sync with their entry, but AI Look Back results and the Memos tab's AI results don't sync.
  • Safety check: Before a look-back, a summary, or a memo AI feature asks the AI, the app first checks on its own for words that could be a sign of risk, and a look-back or summary also stops if the AI judges the danger urgent. If it looks like urgent danger, that feature stops and crisis support information is shown, and nothing the AI made is saved then. What you wrote stays as it is. This check runs only when you use these AI features, and when you write a feeling in your own words in the emotion diary. For a feeling you write yourself, nothing stops: only the "See Where to Reach Out" option appears, and the result of the check is neither saved nor sent.
  • Concern mark: If the AI sees something that isn't urgent but may be weighing on you, a "See Where to Reach Out" option appears too. For a look-back, this safety mark is saved on this device with the look-back result.
  • Not a diagnosis: The AI's look-backs and the feelings it suggests aren't a diagnosis and may not match how you really feel. You can hide a result with "Not Quite — Hide This Reading" (the text stays even when hidden; see section 7).
  • Other processing on the device: Recognizing handwriting (Apple's Vision) and working out which language you wrote in also happen on this device.

5. No external AI in version 1.0

  • The WakaruYo 1.0 app doesn't send your entries or memos to any external AI service or to the developer's servers. The 1.0 app contains no address for connecting to an external AI, so sending to an external AI can't work, and no external AI settings are shown.
  • "Continue in ChatGPT" in the Memos tab isn't the app connecting to an external AI. It's a share: after you check what goes, you hand it yourself, through the iOS share sheet or the clipboard, to an app you choose, and what happens next is up to the app that received it (OpenAI, for ChatGPT) (section 3).
  • If a feature that uses an external AI is added in the future, the app will ask you first, before sending any of your writing. That feature will be released only after this policy has been updated to say what is sent, to whom, and where. If you don't agree, you can keep using the AI on this device only, just as now.

Not part of the app: iOS Writing Tools and the ChatGPT extension

On a device with Apple Intelligence turned on, you can use Writing Tools, which iOS provides, in the app's text fields, including those for entries and memos. If you've turned on the ChatGPT extension in iOS, you can also use ChatGPT from Writing Tools. Both are features Apple builds into iOS, separate from the app's own AI features in section 4.

  • What the app does: The app doesn't turn these features on, and doesn't block them. It never sends your writing through them, and it has no code that reads what they send. Nothing sent with these features reaches the developer. If you keep text that Writing Tools changed in a text field, it's saved just like text you typed yourself. The AI features in section 4 don't go through these features; they use only the on-device model. The ChatGPT extension and "Continue in ChatGPT" in the Memos tab (section 3) are different things: the extension is iOS sending from Writing Tools, while "Continue in ChatGPT" hands text you've checked to the iOS share sheet or the clipboard.
  • Writing Tools: Features such as "Rewrite", "Proofread", and "Summarize" are handled by Apple Intelligence. According to Apple, Apple Intelligence handles many requests entirely on this device, and may send requests that need more computing power to Apple's Private Cloud Compute. Data being processed on Private Cloud Compute isn't stored or made accessible to Apple. For details, see Apple's Apple Intelligence & Privacy. The app's "Summarize This Entry" (AI Summary) and "Summarize" in Writing Tools are different features despite the similar name, and neither uses ChatGPT.
  • The ChatGPT extension starts out off: It's turned on only when you set it up yourself and agree, following the steps iOS shows you. You can turn it on in iOS Settings, and depending on the iOS version, iOS may also ask whether to turn it on the first time you use "Compose" in Writing Tools. You can choose whether to use the ChatGPT extension in Writing Tools separately from other features.
  • You also choose when to send: According to Apple, the Writing Tools feature that uses ChatGPT is "Compose". Something is sent only after you describe what you want, choose what to include (such as "Include All Text"), and tap send. If you choose "Include All Text", all the text in that field (for example, the whole body of an entry or memo) may be included in the request.
  • What goes to ChatGPT: According to Apple, your request and what you attach, plus limited data such as your current time zone, country, device type, language, and the feature you're using. Your IP address is obscured from ChatGPT, but your general location, estimated from it, is provided to ChatGPT for purposes such as preventing fraud and complying with the law. Apple receives limited information, such as the number of requests and the approximate size of each request and response, to run the service and prevent fraud.
  • After it's sent: What goes to ChatGPT is handled by OpenAI. According to Apple, if you aren't signed in to ChatGPT, OpenAI receives no information tied to your Apple Account, must use your information only to fulfill your request, must not store your request or its responses unless the law requires it, and must not use it to improve or train its models. If you're signed in, your ChatGPT account settings and OpenAI's privacy policies apply, and OpenAI may log your requests, attachments, and session history and use them to train or improve its models. For details, see Apple's ChatGPT Extension & Privacy.
  • How to turn it off: You can turn off the ChatGPT extension under ChatGPT in iOS Settings > Siri on iOS 27, or in iOS Settings > Apple Intelligence & Siri > ChatGPT on iOS 18 and iOS 26. You can also block access to the ChatGPT extension in Screen Time.

6. Permissions

  • Camera: used when you take a photo or video for an entry.
  • Microphone: used for sound when you record video, and when you tap "Dictate".
  • Speech Recognition: used when you tap "Dictate". It's processed on this device only.
  • Location (While Using the App): asked only when you turn on "Add the Weather to New Entries". Only a reduced-accuracy location is used, for the weather and city name, and the location isn't stored.
  • Calendars: Full Access is asked for when you turn on "Show Calendar Events on Today and in the Diary" or tap "Allow Full Access" under Calendar in Settings, and "Add Events Only" when you turn on "Let To Dos Go into the Calendar". Adding to-dos works with "Add Events Only".
  • Notifications: asked the first time you turn on a reminder for a to-do.
  • Photo library: never asked. The app receives only what you pick in the iOS photo picker.
  • Clipboard: never asked. The app writes to it only when you tap "Copy Only", or when you touch and hold "Contact" in Settings and choose "Copy Address" (the developer's email address). It reads it only when you tap the iOS Paste button in "Bring Back ChatGPT's Answer". When you select text and use the iOS Copy command, or choose Copy in a share sheet, iOS does the copying.
  • You can change permissions anytime in iOS Settings > WakaruYo. If you decline or take back a permission, only that feature is unavailable, and the rest of the app works as usual.
  • Turning a feature off or taking a permission back stops it from then on. It doesn't delete what was already saved or sent. For that, see section 7.

7. How long data is kept, and how to delete it

The app's data stays on this device until you delete it or delete the app. The developer has no copy, so you do the deleting yourself, as described below. The app can't bring back anything you delete in it. A device backup made before you deleted it, though, still holds it until that backup is deleted. If iCloud sync is on, what you delete on this device is also deleted from iCloud and your other devices (section 3).

Diary

  • Deleting an entry: Its card, handwriting, photos and videos, decorations, weather, page style, summary, AI Look Back result and the text the AI read, and its files and previews are deleted with it. If the entry was moved over from a memo, the Memos tab data from that memo is deleted too, and notes kept with "Keep a Thought Note" remain with their text, marked "Original deleted".
  • To-dos made from that entry remain. Only their link to the entry is removed.
  • Let Go After 24 Hours: 24 hours after you choose this, the entry is hidden, and the next time you open or return to the app it's deleted the same way as when you delete an entry. Entries set to let go are never uploaded to iCloud. A device backup made during those 24 hours may include it, though, and keeps it until that backup is deleted.
  • What remains after letting go: to-dos made from the entry and events added to your calendar, Collection records, stickers saved to My Stickers, and thought notes left marked "Original deleted" (including the text written in them).
  • Even after an entry is deleted or let go, its random ID may remain in the ID lists in section 2. The lists of entries that received a card or had weather tried are never cleaned up, and the list of entries moved over from memos is cleaned up only while iCloud sync runs. These lists contain no text.
  • Things you can delete on their own: a single photo or video, a single decoration, "Remove the Weather from This Entry", "Remove Summary", "Let Go" on a diary card, and the feelings you chose yourself without AI (taking all of them out deletes that record; with an AI Look Back result, only your choice is cleared and the result stays)
  • AI Look Back results: Version 1.0 has no way to delete only the result. "Not Quite — Hide This Reading" only hides it from view, and the result's text and the text the AI read remain. Looking back again replaces it with a new result, and deleting the entry deletes it too.
  • Collection: Version 1.0 has no way to delete Collection records. Deleting an entry doesn't remove the Collection record of its card (card number, when you met it, and how many times). On the device, the records go away when you delete the app, and the iCloud copy can be deleted with "Delete iCloud Copy…".

Memos and the Memos tab

  • Deleting a memo: Its handwriting file, the thought notes you didn't keep, and its AI tidy-up, "Think Further" suggestions, Short Talk, and mind map are deleted with it. Thought notes kept from that memo aren't deleted. They remain, marked "Original deleted". To-dos made from the memo remain too.
  • Deleting a thought note: Its links, its places in structure notes, and the mind-map branches that show it are deleted with it (the branches under them move up a level). Deleting a structure note deletes only the structure note, and the notes in it remain.
  • Things you can delete on their own: "Remove the Tidy-Up", dismissing "Think Further" suggestions, "Delete Branch" in a mind map (the branches under it, and the talk held on it, move up a level), and "Clear All" for recent searches
  • There's no separate way to delete a whole Short Talk or mind map. They're deleted when you delete the memo.

Schedule tab and decorations

  • Deleting a to-do: For a routine, turn off "Repeat as a Routine" first, then delete it. For a plan placed on several days, you can choose "Delete This Day Only", "Delete This and Later Days", or "Delete All". Calendar events you added are deleted with it if the app has Full Access. With "Add Events Only" access, you'll need to delete them yourself in the Calendar app.
  • SEE look-backs: deleted when you empty both fields.
  • This week's goals: each goal can be deleted on its own.
  • My Stickers and My Presets: delete them with "Remove from My Stickers" and "Delete Preset". Stickers saved to My Stickers remain even if you delete the entry they came from.

iCloud

  • Turning sync off leaves your data on this device and in iCloud as it is.
  • "Delete iCloud Copy…": deletes WakaruYo's entire copy in iCloud and turns off sync on this device. Data on this device and your other devices stays, and sync on your other devices stops too.
  • This button appears only while sync is on. If you've already turned sync off, turn it back on and then delete. Keep in mind that turning sync back on uploads this device's data first.
  • The sync key remains in your keychain (and in iCloud Keychain, if it's on) even after you delete the iCloud copy or the app. It's a randomly generated value and contains none of your writing. Version 1.0 has no way to delete this key.

If you delete the app
